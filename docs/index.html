The City Is Lava

Can a squirrel cross Hamburg without touching the ground? Every street tree is a crown (zoom in for 3D); crowns that touch, or are close enough to jump, form a path. Parks, cemeteries and allotments are safe islands. The rest is lava.

Tap a tree or a park to see how far a squirrel gets from there.

Critical gaps · one new tree would join two groups

    How far does jumping help?

    Method and limits

    Trees. Straßenbaumkataster Hamburg: every tree on public streets with a recorded crown diameter (228,091 of 229,013), drawn as a circle. Trees inside parks and on private land are not in the register, so parks count as safe islands instead.

    Links. Hard Mode: crowns touch or overlap. Squirrel Mode: a gap of up to 2 m (a starting assumption to compare, not a measured jump). The slider tries 0–4 m. A crown that reaches a park, cemetery or allotment (Digitaler Grünplan) links to it; touching green areas link.

    Roads. With the switch on, a link whose straight trunk-to-trunk line crosses a major road (OSM) is cut. Crowns can meet above a road, so this is a stricter scenario, not a proof.

    New trees have an 8 m crown. Critical gaps: places where one such tree would link two Squirrel-Mode groups (gaps crossing major roads are skipped).

    Extending the route. The pink rings are up to 5 new trees (8 m crowns) chosen one by one so that the longest canopy path in the largest Squirrel-Mode group grows the most; each joins the group to a tree outside it with gaps of 2 m or less, never across a major road.

    Limits. Crowns are circles from the register's crown diameter; in 3D the crown shape follows the genus (tall oval for limes and hornbeams, broad dome for oaks, planes and chestnuts, narrow column for poplars and birches, cone for conifers, small round for fruit trees), and the heights are a sketch, not data. Buildings are OpenStreetMap heights. A dashed line is a possible jump, not an observed squirrel.
